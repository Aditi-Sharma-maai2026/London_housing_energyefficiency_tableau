# Navigating Inequality: How Economic Disparities Affect London's Housing Landscape

### An exploration of London's housing market through the lens of affordability, property ownership and energy efficiency.

## Overview

Does the London housing market offer equal opportunities to everyone? And how do economic circumstances shape the quality, cost and characteristics of the homes people live in?

This Tableau project investigates the relationship between London's housing market and wider socioeconomic inequalities. By bringing together property transaction data and Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) information, the analysis explores how housing prices, ownership patterns and energy efficiency vary across London.

The aim is to go beyond property prices and uncover the economic and structural factors that shape London's housing landscape.

## Key Areas of Analysis

- **Property Ownership:** Exploring differences between freehold and leasehold properties, including patterns across locations and property categories.
- **Energy Efficiency:** Investigating EPC ratings, window energy performance, property characteristics and their relationship with property prices.
- **Socioeconomic Inequality:** Exploring how levels of deprivation relate to housing and energy efficiency.
- **Market Trends:** Examining changes in property prices over time, including the period surrounding Brexit.
- **Economic Vulnerability:** Considering how wider economic events, including the 2008 financial crisis, relate to housing market conditions.

## Tools & Technologies

- Tableau Public – Data visualisation and interactive dashboards
- Data analysis – Exploration of housing, property and energy performance indicators

## Dataset

The analysis uses London property and energy performance data, with variables including:

- Property transaction prices and dates
- Postcodes and geographical classifications
- Property type, tenure and new-build status
- Floor area and number of rooms
- EPC ratings and energy performance indicators

The dataset contains missing values in some fields, which should be considered when interpreting the visualisations.

## Key Takeaway

Housing inequality is about more than the price of a property. Ownership structures, geographical differences, energy efficiency and wider economic conditions all contribute to the housing experience.

This project uses data visualisation to make these relationships easier to explore and understand.
